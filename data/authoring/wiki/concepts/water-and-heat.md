# Water and heat

*Heating and cooling water, convection, sea and land breezes, water in heat transfer.*

Part of the [[Water]] thread.

**First taught:** Class 6, [[science-vi-ch08|Science · Ch 8 A Journey through States of Water]].

## The idea, class by class

| Class | Subject · chapter | Role | What this chapter says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch04|The World Around Us · Ch 4 Our School—A Happy Place]] | ○ uses | Water kept in the shade of a tree stays cooler than water left in the Sun, and clay matkas cool drinking water without electricity by evaporative cooling. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch08|Science · Ch 8 A Journey through States of Water]] | ● introduces | Supplying heat turns ice to water and water to vapour while removing heat freezes water, and because evaporation takes heat from the water left behind, a porous earthen matka, a pot-in-pot cooler or water sprinkled on a floor cools things down. |
| 7 | [[social_sciences-vii-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Climates of India]] | ● introduces | Because the sea absorbs and releases heat more slowly than land, coastal Mumbai has a yearly temperature range of about 14°C against inland Nagpur's 34°C, and this same land–ocean heating difference creates the pressure contrasts that drive the monsoon winds. |
| 7 | [[science-vii-ch07|Science · Ch 7 Heat Transfer in Nature]] | ◐ extends | Water heats by convection — warmed water at the base expands, rises and is replaced by cooler water, as a potassium permanganate trace shows — and because land heats and cools faster than water, air flows from sea to land by day (sea breeze) and from land to sea at night (land breeze), while a pan of water on a flame uses conduction, convection and radiation at once. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch06|Science · Ch 6 Pressure, Winds, Storms, and Cyclones]] | ◐ extends | Sea and land breezes are re-explained through pressure: by day the faster-heating land warms the air above it into a low-pressure area that cooler sea air flows into, and at night the warmer sea reverses the flow. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch08|Social Sciences · Ch 8 World Geography: Some Glimpses]] | ◐ extends | Oceans regulate climate by absorbing heat and releasing it slowly, and warm currents such as the Gulf Stream carry equatorial heat north through the Atlantic so that the United Kingdom is warmer than other places at the same latitude. |
| 9 | [[science-ix-ch13|Science · Ch 13 Earth as a System: Energy, Matter, and Life]] | ◐ extends | Water shapes Earth's heat budget: water vapour is a greenhouse gas, dark ocean water (low albedo) absorbs heat while snow and ice reflect it, and ocean currents move heat from the equator toward the poles, regulating climate. |
| 9 | [[social_sciences-ix-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Atmosphere and Climate]] | ◐ extends | Land heats and cools faster than the sea, so by day a sea breeze blows from sea to land and at night a land breeze from land to sea, moderating coastal climates, and the same contrast on a seasonal scale drives the monsoon's reversal. |
