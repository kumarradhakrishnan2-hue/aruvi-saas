# Rivers and landforms

*Rivers from source to sea, erosion and deposition, valleys, deltas, floods, glaciers.*

Part of the [[Water]] thread.

**First taught:** Class 5, [[the_world_around_us-v-ch01|The World Around Us · Ch 1 Water — The Essence of Life]].

## The idea, class by class

| Class | Subject · chapter | Role | What this chapter says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch01|The World Around Us · Ch 1 Water — The Essence of Life]] | ● introduces | Rivers begin in mountains and flow downhill with their path and speed shaped by the land they cross (and flowing water reshapes land over time), modelled with mustard seeds on crumpled paper and traced on a map of India's rivers, including the Luni that ends in the Rann of Kutch without reaching a sea. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch02|The World Around Us · Ch 2 Journey of a River]] | ◐ extends | One river is followed source to sea: the Godavari rises as a stream at Trimbakeshwar in the Western Ghats, gathers tributaries over 1,465 km, flows year-round (perennial, unlike seasonal rivers), spreads into a delta where freshwater meets the sea at the Coringa mangroves, and overflows its banks in floods when rain is excessive — worse where forests have been cut. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Landforms and Life]] | ● introduces | Snow melting on mountains feeds rivers such as the 2,500 km Ganga with its Himalayan tributaries, rivers plunge off plateau edges as waterfalls (Jog, Hundru, Nohkalikai, Victoria), and rivers rising in mountains carry rock, sand and silt down to deposit them as the fertile soils of floodplains. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch06|Social Sciences · Ch 6 The Beginnings of Indian Civilisation]] | ○ uses | Settlements were placed along rivers both for water and for the fertile soil rivers create, and the Sarasvatī — today the seasonal Ghaggar in India and Hakra in Pakistan — is a river that later texts describe drying up and disappearing. |
| 7 | [[science-vii-ch05|Science · Ch 5 Changes Around Us: Physical and Chemical]] | ● introduces | Rocks break into sediments by weathering — water freezing in cracks, temperature swings, growing roots, and water with dissolved chemicals reacting with rock surfaces — and flowing water and wind then carry the sediments away (erosion) to settle in water bodies where, over thousands of years, they harden into new rock. |
| 7 | [[social_sciences-vii-ch01|Social Sciences · Ch 1 Geographical Diversity of India]] | ◐ extends | Summer snow-melt from the Himalayas — the Bhagirathi rising at Gaumukh from the Gangotri Glacier — feeds the Ganga, Indus and Brahmaputra whose mineral-laden waters build the fertile Gangetic plains, the eastward tilt of the peninsular plateau sends the Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri and Mahanadi to the Bay of Bengal as wide fertile deltas while the Narmada and Tapti flow west into estuaries, and the Ganga–Brahmaputra delta forms the mangrove Sundarbans. |
| 7 | [[social_sciences-vii-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Climates of India]] | ○ uses | Floods occur when heavy rain, river-bank breaches or bursting glacial lakes send water over normally dry land — as in the 2013 Kedarnath disaster — and urban flooding is worsened by poor drainage, encroached waterways and impermeable concrete. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch08|Social Sciences · Ch 8 World Geography: Some Glimpses]] | ○ uses | Mountains act as 'water towers' — the Tibetan 'Third Pole' sources the Indus, Brahmaputra and Yangtze and its snow-melt is now expanding lakes, the Alps feed the Rhine and Danube, the Andes feed the Amazon (the largest river by volume) — while plains such as the European Plain are built by sediment deposition and the Colorado Plateau's Grand Canyon is cut by erosion. |
| 9 | [[social_sciences-ix-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Shaping of the Earth's Surface]] | ◐ extends | A river shapes land differently along its course — V-shaped valleys, waterfalls and rapids where hard rock resists in the upper course, meanders and oxbow lakes from outer-bank erosion and inner-bank deposition in the middle, deltas, levees and alluvial fans where it meets the sea — while glaciers carve U-shaped valleys, cirques and fjords, leave moraines that dam lakes, and can unleash Glacial Lake Outburst Floods when their ice or moraine dams fail. |
| 9 | [[science-ix-ch13|Science · Ch 13 Earth as a System: Energy, Matter, and Life]] | ○ uses | Melting glaciers add water to rivers and raise sea levels that threaten coastal cities such as Mumbai and Chennai, and intense rainfall runoff erodes soil. |
| 9 | [[social_sciences-ix-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Atmosphere and Climate]] | ○ uses | The 2025 Punjab floods came from heavy monsoon rain swelling the Satluj, Beas, Ravi and Ghaggar in succession, made worse by weak old embankments (dhūsī bāndh), building too close to rivers, silt reducing river and dam capacity and late warnings — and climate change is making floods, droughts, glacier melt and sea-level rise more frequent. |
