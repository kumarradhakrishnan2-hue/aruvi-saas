# States of water

*Solid, liquid and gas; melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation, boiling.*

Part of the [[Water]] thread.

**First taught:** Class 6, [[science-vi-ch08|Science · Ch 8 A Journey through States of Water]].

## The idea, class by class

| Class | Subject · chapter | Role | What this chapter says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch01|The World Around Us · Ch 1 Water — The Essence of Life]] | ◐ extends | Water exists as liquid (rain), solid (ice) and vapour (steam); students watch droplets condense on a cold steel glass of ice, ice melt, water boil and water left in sunlight for three days disappear, recording that water changes form and can return to liquid. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch08|Science · Ch 8 A Journey through States of Water]] | ● introduces | Water exists as ice, liquid water and water vapour; evaporation (which goes on even at room temperature) and condensation, melting and freezing move it between these states, and evaporation is faster with more exposed surface, higher temperature, moving air and drier air. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch07|Science · Ch 7 Temperature and its Measurement]] | ○ uses | Boiling water and melting ice each hold a constant temperature, and students' readings of boiling water in Shillong come out slightly below 100 °C, raising the idea that the boiling point of water depends on altitude. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch09|Science · Ch 9 Methods of Separation in Everyday Life]] | ○ uses | Evaporation — a liquid turning into its vapour — is put to work to separate a dissolved solid from water, whether by sunlight and air over seawater in shallow pits or by boiling a salt solution away over a spirit lamp. |
| 7 | [[science-vii-ch05|Science · Ch 5 Changes Around Us: Physical and Chemical]] | ○ uses | Ice melting, water boiling or evaporating and wet clothes drying are physical changes — the water stays water in all three states — and they are reversible, since melted ice can be refrozen and evaporated water condensed, unlike burning wood or making popcorn. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch07|Science · Ch 7 Particulate Nature of Matter]] | ◐ extends | Ice, water and vapour are explained by particles: in ice they only vibrate in fixed positions, at the melting point (0 °C) they break free to move within a limited space, at the boiling point they escape as vapour with bubbles forming throughout the bulk, whereas evaporation happens only at the surface at any temperature — and water, unlike air, is practically incompressible. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch09|Science · Ch 9 The Amazing World of Solutes, Solvents, and Solutions]] | ◐ extends | Water is densest at 4 °C and expands as it freezes into ice at 0 °C, so ice is less dense than liquid water and floats — the anomalous behaviour of water. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch13|Science · Ch 13 Our Home: Earth, a Unique Life Sustaining Planet]] | ○ uses | Earth sits in the habitable (Goldilocks) zone where temperatures keep water liquid — closer to the Sun it would evaporate, farther away it would freeze — and the atmosphere's mild greenhouse effect is what holds that liquid range. |
| 9 | [[science-ix-ch05|Science · Ch 5 Exploring Mixtures and their Separation]] | ○ uses | Distillation exploits water's 100 °C boiling point — a lower-boiling liquid like acetone (56 °C) is boiled off first and condensed in a water-cooled condenser — and clouds of water droplets or ice crystals in air are posed as a colloid puzzle, with snowflakes and frost cited as natural crystals. |
| 9 | [[social_sciences-ix-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Atmosphere and Climate]] | ○ uses | Water vapour makes up only about 0.1 to 0.4 per cent of the atmosphere yet drives cloud formation, warmer air holds more of it, and precipitation is that vapour condensing out of saturated air as drizzle, rain, snow, sleet or hail. |
