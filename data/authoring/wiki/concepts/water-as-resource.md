# Water as a resource

*Sources, scarcity, conservation, rainwater harvesting, wells, tanks, dams, irrigation, supply.*

Part of the [[Water]] thread.

**First taught:** Class 3, [[the_world_around_us-iii-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Water–A Precious Gift]].

## The idea, class by class

| Class | Subject · chapter | Role | What this chapter says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | [[the_world_around_us-iii-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Water–A Precious Gift]] | ● introduces | Tap water comes from rooftop tanks, pipes, wells, borewells or tankers; many people must carry water from a distant well, river or lake; households store water against irregular supply; and water is precious, so it must not be wasted or polluted. |
| 4 | [[mathematics-iv-ch08|Mathematics · Ch 8 Weigh It, Pour It]] | ○ uses | Students count the litres of water they drink and use each day for showering, washing clothes and watering crops, and collect the drip from a leaking tap for an hour to work out how much water is wasted in a day and a week. |
| 4 | [[the_world_around_us-iv-ch09|The World Around Us · Ch 9 Different Lands, Different Lives]] | ○ uses | Water is scarce in the desert, so plants like cactus, khejri and babool survive by storing water and desert houses keep tankas (water tanks) for storage; students discuss why deserts are short of water. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch01|The World Around Us · Ch 1 Water — The Essence of Life]] | ● introduces | Rainwater percolates through soil and rock to become groundwater drawn up by wells, borewells, tube wells and handpumps; paved city surfaces block recharge while open ground, soak pits, ponds and trees restore it, and students design a house or school that conserves water. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch02|The World Around Us · Ch 2 Journey of a River]] | ◐ extends | A river supplies water for drinking, cooking, washing, irrigation and industry through pipes and canals; India's 900-plus dams store river water for dry seasons, irrigation and electricity at the cost of flooding land and displacing people; and drought (Chennai 2019) forces migration and calls for conservation habits, the National Water Mission and water ATMs. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch04|The World Around Us · Ch 4 Our School—A Happy Place]] | ◐ extends | A dripping tap wastes far more water than it seems — students time how long a drip takes to fill a glass and scale it up to a day — and a green school fixes leaks, prevents wastage and collects rainwater, in the spirit of the Jal Shakti Abhiyan. |
| 5 | [[english-v-ch06|English · Ch 6 What a Tank!]] | ○ uses | Water has been stored for centuries in structures like Tamil Nadu's ooranis for rainwater harvesting, Kerala's year-round Panam Keni wells and Rajasthan's underground tankas, and today in household overhead tanks — and children list how water is wasted daily, how to stop it, and hear how a day without supply led a family to set up rainwater harvesting at home. |
| 5 | [[english-v-ch08|English · Ch 8 The Decision of the Panchayat]] | ○ uses | A farmer buys the well beside his new field because the well is the field's source of water, and the chapter states outright that water is priceless — so valuable that no price can be put on it. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Energy—How Things Work]] | ○ uses | Flowing water is a clean energy source: fast-moving rivers generate electricity and traditional gharaats (watermills) in hilly areas grind grain, modelled with a paper-cup water wheel that turns when water is poured on it — placed under the nearest id, since hydropower is not in the vocabulary. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch11|Science · Ch 11 Nature's Treasures]] | ● introduces | Water is a precious resource because most of it is saline and only a very small fraction of freshwater is easily accessible, so wastage in hand washing, cooking and gardening should be cut by turning off taps, fixing leaks, recycling water and harvesting rainwater — access is unequal across India, and water counts as a renewable resource only when used judiciously. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch08|Science · Ch 8 A Journey through States of Water]] | ○ uses | Only a small fraction of Earth's water is available to living things, the rest lying in the oceans, so water must be used wisely and water bodies kept free of pollution; an Atmospheric Water Generator can even harvest water by condensing humid air. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Landforms and Life]] | ○ uses | Farming on the Ganga plain, once rainfed, now depends heavily on irrigation from canals and groundwater, which has raised production but depleted groundwater — a challenge for the future. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch06|Social Sciences · Ch 6 The Beginnings of Indian Civilisation]] | ○ uses | Harappans drew water from brick-lined wells, ponds and streams, and where these were lacking built human-made reservoirs that harvested and distributed water — an infrastructure that needed organised administration to build and maintain. |
| 7 | [[science-vii-ch07|Science · Ch 7 Heat Transfer in Nature]] | ◐ extends | Rain seeps into the ground fastest through gravel and slowest through clay (infiltration) to become groundwater held in aquifers that wells and bore wells tap, and since over-extraction and urban concrete are depleting it, rainwater harvesting and recharge pits are needed — Ladakh's ice stupas store winter stream water as ice to release for farming in summer. |
| 7 | [[social_sciences-vii-ch01|Social Sciences · Ch 1 Geographical Diversity of India]] | ◐ extends | The Himalayas are the 'Water Tower of Asia' whose rivers supply drinking, farming and industrial water to hundreds of millions, while in the water-scarce Thar people conserve rain with traditional taanka or kund rainwater-harvesting systems. |
| 7 | [[social_sciences-vii-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Climates of India]] | ○ uses | The arid Thar demands unique water conservation practices, and a failed monsoon brings water scarcity that hurts farming and the industries that depend on predictable water availability. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch01|Social Sciences · Ch 1 Natural Resources and Their Use]] | ◐ extends | Water is a renewable resource only while Nature's rhythm of restoration is not disturbed: farmers across India pump groundwater for irrigation faster than it recharges so water tables fall, Punjab's Green Revolution crops and free electricity have left almost 80 per cent of its area 'overexploited' with groundwater beyond about 30 metres, Himalayan glaciers melt faster than precipitation replaces them, and the remedies are water harvesting, pond rejuvenation and reduced consumption. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch14|Social Sciences · Ch 14 India's Urban Landscape]] | ◐ extends | Bengaluru suffers summer water shortages and monsoon flooding because of poor planning, groundwater overuse, no rainwater harvesting and encroached lake beds — its roughly 1,000 lakes reduced to about 200 — and non-profits, resident associations and communities have revived lakes such as Yelahanka by desilting, repairing bunds, clearing drainage channels and replanting, increasing water availability. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch06|Science · Ch 6 Pressure, Winds, Storms, and Cyclones]] | ○ uses | Overhead water tanks are placed high to supply good pressure and dam bases are built broader than their tops to withstand the large sideways water pressure near the bottom, because liquid pressure depends on the height of the water column and acts in all directions (liquid pressure itself has no id; placed under the nearest). |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch08|Social Sciences · Ch 8 World Geography: Some Glimpses]] | ○ uses | Rivers, mountains and lakes are freshwater suppliers — the Nile's irrigation, drinking water and hydropower, the Alps' drinking water, irrigation and hydroelectricity, the Andes as a source for millions, the Great Lakes' city water now facing chemical contamination — while desert life clusters at Sahara oases and Gobi pastures fed by underground water. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch10|Social Sciences · Ch 10 A Journey Through Indian Architecture]] | ○ uses | Because India's monsoon delivers water seasonally, people over millennia built structures to harvest, store and distribute it — from the village pond to the Grand Anicut — suited to each region's geology and climate, and many now suffer from depleted sources, poor maintenance, pollution and waste dumping that environmentalists, locals and NGOs work to reverse. |
| 9 | [[social_sciences-ix-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Shaping of the Earth's Surface]] | ○ uses | Rivers, glaciers and groundwater are used as well as feared: the Grand Anicut (Kallanai) in Tamil Nadu shows rivers harnessed for irrigation, moraine-dammed lakes give water supply, irrigation and hydropower, karst provides fresh water, the Sindhu-Sarasvatī people used contouring, bunding, terracing, dams and canals, and Nagaland's Zabo system of earthen bunds and check dams conserves soil and water and recharges groundwater. |
