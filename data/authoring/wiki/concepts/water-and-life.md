# Water and life

*Water in the body, in plants, aquatic habitats, drinking water, health and sanitation.*

Part of the [[Water]] thread.

**First taught:** Class 5, [[the_world_around_us-v-ch01|The World Around Us · Ch 1 Water — The Essence of Life]].

## The idea, class by class

| Class | Subject · chapter | Role | What this chapter says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | [[english-iii-ch13|English · Ch 13 Madhu's Wish]] | ○ uses | When hiccups strike, sharbat, kheer and badam milk cannot stop them and only a jug of water can, so children talk about why the body needs water above all other food. |
| 3 | [[the_world_around_us-iii-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Water–A Precious Gift]] | ○ uses | Water is essential for the survival of people, birds and animals, so students build and maintain a birdbath, note which birds and insects come to drink, and ask whether the water near them is fit for drinking. |
| 4 | [[the_world_around_us-iv-ch09|The World Around Us · Ch 9 Different Lands, Different Lives]] | ○ uses | The sea along the Odisha coast is a habitat for sea turtles, crabs, dolphins, corals, seaweed and starfish, which students identify by name from pictures. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch01|The World Around Us · Ch 1 Water — The Essence of Life]] | ● introduces | All living beings depend on freshwater, and freshwater habitats hold specially adapted plants and animals (fins and gills, waxy leaves; dragonfly, pond heron, freshwater turtle, lotus, water hyacinth) linked in a food chain, which students observe on a visit to a pond, lake or aquarium. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch02|The World Around Us · Ch 2 Journey of a River]] | ◐ extends | Waste, plastic, untreated sewage and fertiliser runoff make river water unsafe for people and wildlife — algal 'green blankets' choke aquatic life and make water undrinkable — and floods spread disease and cut off safe drinking water. |
| 5 | [[english-v-ch05|English · Ch 5 The Frog]] | ○ uses | A frog lives in water and on land alike — sitting in ponds and muddy drains, swimming first as a tadpole and later with webbed feet — and children compare this amphibious life with a fish's and draw the frog's pond home. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch10|Science · Ch 10 Living Creatures: Exploring their Characteristics]] | ● introduces | A seed needs water and air (not light) to germinate — water softens the seed coat so the embryo can grow — while mosquitoes lay eggs on stagnant water where larvae and pupae surface to breathe, and frogs begin life as spawn and tadpoles swimming in ponds before living on both land and water. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Oceans and Continents]] | ● introduces | Every layer of the ocean from sunlit surface to dark depths hosts marine flora (algae, seaweeds) and marine fauna (fish, dolphins, whales), the ocean's plant life makes more than half the world's oxygen, and salty seawater is unfit for land animals to drink. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch06|Social Sciences · Ch 6 The Beginnings of Indian Civilisation]] | ○ uses | Harappan homes had bathing areas connected to a city-wide drainage network beneath the streets that carried waste water away — an early system of sanitation. |
| 7 | [[science-vii-ch10|Science · Ch 10 Life Processes in Plants]] | ◐ extends | Plants need water to grow (a sapling in sunlight without water fails), water is a raw material of photosynthesis (carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen), and roots absorb water with dissolved minerals that travel upward through xylem tubes, as a stem cutting in red-inked water shows. |
| 7 | [[science-vii-ch09|Science · Ch 9 Nutrition and Respiration in Animals (summary carries no chapter title; named from its sections 9.1 and 9.2)]] | ○ uses | The large intestine absorbs water from undigested food, respiration in cells releases water along with carbon dioxide and energy, and fish breathe by passing dissolved oxygen from the surrounding water into the blood through gills, while tadpoles use gills and frogs and earthworms exchange gases through moist skin. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch13|Science · Ch 13 Our Home: Earth, a Unique Life Sustaining Planet]] | ◐ extends | Liquid water is essential for life to evolve and be sustained: it acts as a solvent, transports nutrients in plants, regulates body temperature in animals, aids digestion and supports aquatic life from plankton to whales, while water pollution from industrial, agricultural and plastic waste harms that life. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch08|Social Sciences · Ch 8 World Geography: Some Glimpses]] | ◐ extends | Oceans support life from plankton to the blue whale and supply almost half the oxygen we breathe, trench organisms live in total darkness on chemical energy instead of photosynthesis, and coral reefs — 'rainforests of the sea' sheltering a quarter of all ocean species — are bleaching as human actions change ocean chemistry. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch09|Science · Ch 9 The Amazing World of Solutes, Solvents, and Solutions]] | ○ uses | Colder water holds more dissolved oxygen, so warming water leaves fish short of oxygen, and because ice is less dense than water it floats and insulates the water beneath, keeping aquatic life alive in cold climates. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch01|Social Sciences · Ch 1 Natural Resources and Their Use]] | ○ uses | Water, with air and food, is a resource life cannot exist without and humans cannot manufacture, yet chemical contamination of over-pumped groundwater in Punjab and cement-industry pollution now pose health hazards, and access to water is inequitably distributed with the poor bearing the burden. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch14|Social Sciences · Ch 14 India's Urban Landscape]] | ○ uses | Unplanned urban growth and slums leave residents with poor access to water and sanitation, while sewage- and chemical-polluted lakes such as Bellandur and Varthur foam, release toxic gases and even catch fire. |
| 9 | [[science-ix-ch02|Science · Ch 2 Cell: The Building Block of Life]] | ◐ extends | Water moves into and out of cells by osmosis — diffusion across the selectively permeable membrane from a dilute to a concentrated solution — so a potato piece or cheek cell shrinks in 20% salt or sugar solution and a plant cell plasmolyses while its wall holds its outer shape, and the water-filled central vacuole's turgor keeps plant cells firm until water deficit makes the plant wilt. |
| 9 | [[science-ix-ch03|Science · Ch 3 Tissues in Action]] | ◐ extends | Water enters through root hairs that enlarge the root's absorbing surface, rises through xylem's dead tracheids and vessels, and leaves through stomata as transpiration — a loss the waxy cuticle limits (thicker in dry habitats) but which also creates the transpiration pull that keeps water moving upward against gravity. |
| 9 | [[science-ix-ch12|Science · Ch 12 Patterns in Life: Diversity and Classification]] | ○ uses | The plant kingdom is ordered by its dependence on water: thallophytes are aquatic, bryophytes and pteridophytes live on land but still need water for their swimming male gametes, and gymnosperms are the first to reproduce without water — while sponges feed on water flowing through their pores and fins and gills fit fish for aquatic life. |
| 9 | [[science-ix-ch13|Science · Ch 13 Earth as a System: Energy, Matter, and Life]] | ○ uses | Excess nitrate fertiliser washed into rivers and lakes triggers algal blooms that deplete oxygen and kill fish (eutrophication), and warmer, more acidic seawater threatens plankton and coral reefs. |
