# Measuring water

*Capacity and volume in litres, weighing liquids, measuring water temperature.*

Part of the [[Water]] thread.

**First taught:** Class 3, [[mathematics-iii-ch11|Mathematics · Ch 11 Filling and Lifting]].

## The idea, class by class

| Class | Subject · chapter | Role | What this chapter says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | [[mathematics-iii-ch11|Mathematics · Ch 11 Filling and Lifting]] | ● introduces | How much a vessel holds cannot be judged by its shape, so water or milk is poured into same-sized glasses to compare fairly, and the litre (then half and quarter litre) is met as the standard unit by checking a bucket, mug and glass against a 1-litre bottle. |
| 3 | [[the_world_around_us-iii-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Water–A Precious Gift]] | ○ uses | Students estimate how much water everyday tasks need in informal units (mugs of water to brush teeth, water used for a shower) to build a sense of quantity — placed under the nearest id, since no standard units such as litres are used. |
| 4 | [[mathematics-iv-ch08|Mathematics · Ch 8 Weigh It, Pour It]] | ◐ extends | The litre is subdivided into millilitres — 500 ml is half a litre, 250 ml a quarter, 100 ml a tenth — discovered by pouring, with 10 ml dosing cups and 1 ml droppers for tiny amounts, and household containers (water bottle, glass, mug, jug, bucket) are estimated then measured. |
| 5 | [[mathematics-v-ch08|Mathematics · Ch 8 Weight and Capacity]] | ◐ extends | Litres and millilitres are tied by 1,000 ml = 1 l on a double number line, so 1 l 400 ml is 1,400 ml (not 1,040 ml), and amounts of water, milk, fuel and oil in compound l-and-ml form are compared, added, subtracted, multiplied and divided with regrouping across the 1,000. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch04|The World Around Us · Ch 4 Our School—A Happy Place]] | ○ uses | Water is measured two ways: the time a drip takes to fill a glass (scaled to glasses per day), and water temperature read with a thermometer before and after an hour under a tree versus in the Sun. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch06|Science · Ch 6 Materials Around Us]] | ● introduces | The amount of water in a tumbler is its volume — the space it occupies — and bottles labelled 1 L, 500 mL or 200 mL state that volume in litres and millilitres (with 1 m³ = 1000 L noted as the SI relation). |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch07|Science · Ch 7 Temperature and its Measurement]] | ● introduces | A laboratory thermometer is dipped into warm water and read while still immersed, held vertically with the eye level to the liquid column, to measure the water's temperature in degrees Celsius. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch09|Science · Ch 9 The Amazing World of Solutes, Solvents, and Solutions]] | ◐ extends | Water volume is read on a measuring cylinder (5 mL to 500 mL sizes) at its least count with the eye level to the meniscus — Activity 9.5 measures out exactly 50 mL — and the rise in water level when an irregular object is submerged gives that object's volume, while a digital balance with tare gives mass in grams. |
