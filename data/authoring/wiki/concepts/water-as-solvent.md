# Water as a solvent

*Dissolving, solutions, mixtures, purification and separation.*

Part of the [[Water]] thread.

**First taught:** Class 5, [[the_world_around_us-v-ch02|The World Around Us · Ch 2 Journey of a River]].

## The idea, class by class

| Class | Subject · chapter | Role | What this chapter says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch02|The World Around Us · Ch 2 Journey of a River]] | ● introduces | Some pollutants dissolve invisibly in water while others such as oil do not (a spoon of sugar versus a spoon of cooking oil stirred into two glasses), both harming the river, and students design a simple water filter from everyday materials. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch06|Science · Ch 6 Materials Around Us]] | ● introduces | Stirring sugar, salt, chalk powder, sand and sawdust into water shows that some materials dissolve completely (soluble) while others do not (insoluble), some liquids mix with water while others form a separate layer, and gases such as oxygen dissolve too, which is what makes aquatic life possible. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch09|Science · Ch 9 Methods of Separation in Everyday Life]] | ◐ extends | A dissolved solid such as salt is recovered from water by evaporating the water (in salt pans and by heating a salt solution to dryness), insoluble particles are removed by letting them settle and decanting or by filtering muddy water through filter paper, cloth, cotton, sand or charcoal, and oil is decanted off water because it forms a separate layer. |
| 7 | [[science-vii-ch04|Science · Ch 4 The World of Metals and Non-metals]] | ○ uses | Iron rusts only when both water and air are present (a nail in boiled, oil-covered water or in dry air stays clean), sodium is stored in kerosene to keep it from water while phosphorus is stored under water, and sulfur dioxide dissolves in water to give an acidic solution — water here is a reactant and storage medium, which has no id of its own, so it is placed under the nearest (water-as-solvent). |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch07|Science · Ch 7 Particulate Nature of Matter]] | ◐ extends | Sugar dissolved in water turns invisible but is still there (its taste proves it) because its particles slip into the spaces between water particles, so the solution's volume is less than the sum of the two, whereas insoluble sand settles and adds volume; a grain of potassium permanganate spreading through water — faster in hot water — shows that water particles are always moving. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch09|Science · Ch 9 The Amazing World of Solutes, Solvents, and Solutions]] | ◐ extends | In a salt–water solution the dissolved solid is the solute and water the solvent; a fixed volume of water dissolves only so much salt before the solution is saturated, solubility of most solids rises with temperature (baking soda at 20, 50 and 70 °C) while that of gases falls, and concentration separates dilute from concentrated solutions. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch08|Science · Ch 8 Nature of Matter: Elements, Compounds, and Mixtures]] | ○ uses | Sugar dissolved in water is the model uniform mixture (components indistinguishable even under a microscope), sand-in-water and seawater are solid–liquid mixtures and oxygen-in-water a gas–liquid one, while water itself is shown by passing electricity through it to be a compound of hydrogen and oxygen in a fixed 2:1 ratio — a fact about water's own make-up with no id of its own, placed here as nearest. |
| 9 | [[science-ix-ch05|Science · Ch 5 Exploring Mixtures and their Separation]] | ◐ extends | Water solutions are now quantified — concentration as % m/m, % m/v or % v/v (ORS and 0.9% saline must be exact) and solubility read from curves against temperature — and separated by crystallizing a cooled saturated solution (copper sulfate; salt from seawater brine), distilling off a lower-boiling liquid such as acetone or recovering the solvent, running ink up chromatography paper with water as the solvent, draining oil from water in a separating funnel, and clearing suspended particles by filtration, centrifugation or alum coagulation, while colloids such as milk scatter light. |
| 9 | [[science-ix-ch09|Science · Ch 9 Atomic Foundations of Matter]] | ◐ extends | Ionic compounds such as salt and copper sulfate dissolve in water (not in kerosene or petrol) and their solutions conduct electricity because the ions become free to move, sugar dissolves without conducting, and camphor and naphthalene do not dissolve at all — while water itself is treated as a compound (hydrogen and oxygen always 1:8 by mass whatever the source, two single covalent bonds in H₂O, molecular mass 18 u), a fact with no id of its own placed here as nearest. |
| 9 | [[social_sciences-ix-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Shaping of the Earth's Surface]] | ○ uses | Chemical weathering changes minerals by reaction with water and acids, and groundwater dissolving limestone creates karst — caves, stalactites, stalagmites, sinkholes and underground rivers. |
