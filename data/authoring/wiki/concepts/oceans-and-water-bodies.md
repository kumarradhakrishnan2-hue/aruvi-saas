# Oceans and water bodies

*Oceans, seas, lakes, ponds; how water is distributed on Earth; tides and currents.*

Part of the [[Water]] thread.

**First taught:** Class 3, [[the_world_around_us-iii-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Water–A Precious Gift]].

## The idea, class by class

| Class | Subject · chapter | Role | What this chapter says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | [[the_world_around_us-iii-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Water–A Precious Gift]] | ● introduces | Streams, rivers, ponds and lakes are the local water bodies where rainwater collects, and students find one near their village or city, visit and draw it, and record whether its water is (or was) used for drinking. |
| 4 | [[the_world_around_us-iv-ch09|The World Around Us · Ch 9 Different Lands, Different Lives]] | ○ uses | The sea defines the coastal landform: Odisha's coast has beaches, fishing as the main livelihood, sand art and conch shells, and students compare life by the sea with life in the desert and discuss why beaches must be kept clean. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch01|The World Around Us · Ch 1 Water — The Essence of Life]] | ● introduces | Most of Earth's surface is covered by water but nearly all of it is salty ocean water unfit for drinking or farming; freshwater is only a tiny fraction (a teaspoon in a glass) held in rivers, ponds, lakes and underground, and India's rivers drain either to the Bay of Bengal or the Arabian Sea. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch11|Science · Ch 11 Nature's Treasures]] | ● introduces | Water covers about two-thirds of the Earth's surface, but most of it lies in oceans and seas as saline water, with freshwater held in ice sheets and snow, rivers, lakes, ponds, wells and underground sources. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Oceans and Continents]] | ● introduces | Water covers almost three-fourths of the Earth, mostly as five named but continuously connected oceans (Pacific the largest, Arctic the smallest) whose seas, bays and gulfs are parts of one global body, the Southern Hemisphere is mostly water, and freshwater is only a small share found in glaciers, rivers, lakes, the atmosphere and underground. |
| 7 | [[social_sciences-vii-ch01|Social Sciences · Ch 1 Geographical Diversity of India]] | ○ uses | The Arabian Sea, Indian Ocean and Bay of Bengal bound the subcontinent and hold its two island groups, and India's inland water bodies range from Ladakh's saltwater Pangong Tso to the east-coast Chilika Lake and Pulicat lagoon. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch13|Science · Ch 13 Our Home: Earth, a Unique Life Sustaining Planet]] | ◐ extends | Water covering about 70 per cent of Earth's surface — ponds, rivers, seas, oceans and groundwater — forms the hydrosphere, which is why Earth looks like the Blue Planet from space, and melting ice caps now raise sea levels that flood coastal areas. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch08|Social Sciences · Ch 8 World Geography: Some Glimpses]] | ◐ extends | The ocean covers about 71 per cent of the Earth and holds 97 per cent of its water over a floor of mountains, valleys and trenches (the Mariana's Challenger Deep over 11,000 m down); the five oceans are revisited with their distinct features, ocean currents run like giant rivers carrying warm water poleward and cold water toward the equator, seas, gulfs, bays, straits and canals are classified by shape and trade role, and the Great Lakes are a chain of five massive freshwater lakes. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch06|Science · Ch 6 Pressure, Winds, Storms, and Cyclones]] | ○ uses | A cyclone is powered by warm ocean water — rising moist air condenses and releases latent heat that deepens the low pressure — weakens once it reaches land and loses its moist air, and drives storm surges of 3–12 metres that flood coasts and contaminate drinking water with seawater. |
| 9 | [[science-ix-ch13|Science · Ch 13 Earth as a System: Energy, Matter, and Life]] | ◐ extends | Surface water and groundwater form the hydrosphere and ice and snow the cryosphere; ocean currents driven by planetary winds, temperature and salinity differences, Earth's rotation and the continents form gyres that carry heat poleward (the North Atlantic Drift keeps European ports ice-free) and nutrients to marine life, and the ocean holds 71% of global carbon, acidifying as it warms. |
| 9 | [[social_sciences-ix-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Shaping of the Earth's Surface]] | ○ uses | Sea waves and currents are agents of gradation that undercut headlands into cliffs, wave-cut platforms, caves, arches and stacks and deposit beaches and sand bars that shield settlements from coastal erosion. |
