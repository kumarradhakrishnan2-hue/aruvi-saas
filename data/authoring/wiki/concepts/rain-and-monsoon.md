# Rain and the monsoon

*Rainfall, monsoon winds, seasons and climate patterns, humidity, weather measurement.*

Part of the [[Water]] thread.

**First taught:** Class 3, [[the_world_around_us-iii-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Water–A Precious Gift]].

## The idea, class by class

| Class | Subject · chapter | Role | What this chapter says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | [[the_world_around_us-iii-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Water–A Precious Gift]] | ● introduces | Rain is met by direct observation: children read sky and nature clues to predict whether it will rain, then watch a shower and describe its heaviness, drop size, speed and slant, and collect rainwater to see whether it looks clean or dirty. |
| 4 | [[the_world_around_us-iv-ch09|The World Around Us · Ch 9 Different Lands, Different Lives]] | ○ uses | Rainfall differs across landforms and shapes how people live: deserts get very little rain, Sikkim's heavy rain calls for wooden houses with slanting roofs, the rainy season brings landslides in the mountains, and coastal Odisha faces cyclones. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch02|The World Around Us · Ch 2 Journey of a River]] | ○ uses | Rainfall governs a river's year: seasonal rivers flow only in the rainy season, excessive rain makes rivers overflow their banks, and dams hold water back for the dry season. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch07|Science · Ch 7 Temperature and its Measurement]] | ● introduces | Air temperature is a weather measurement: weather services record each day's maximum and minimum air temperature, which rises towards summer and falls in winter and is used in forecasting (this node is weather measurement only, with no water content — recorded because the vocabulary lists weather measurement here). |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch08|Science · Ch 8 A Journey through States of Water]] | ● introduces | Humidity is the measure of water vapour in the air; on rainy, humid days evaporation is slower and clothes dry slowly, and rain is what falls when cloud droplets grow heavy. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Oceans and Continents]] | ○ uses | India's summer monsoon rains originate over the ocean, and the same oceans generate cyclones — violent storms with extreme rainfall and strong winds that damage coastal regions. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Landforms and Life]] | ○ uses | Precipitation is defined as water reaching the ground as rain, snow or hail, deserts are the landform that receives very little of it, and cloudbursts, flash floods and heavy snowfall are among the hazards of mountain life. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch06|Social Sciences · Ch 6 The Beginnings of Indian Civilisation]] | ○ uses | A global climatic change from about 2200 BCE reduced rainfall and made farming harder, which together with the drying of the Sarasvatī in its central basin led to the abandonment of cities such as Kalibangan and Banawali. |
| 7 | [[social_sciences-vii-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Understanding the Weather]] | ● introduces | Precipitation (rain, snow, sleet or hail) is measured with a rain gauge whose scale reads rainfall in millimetres, humidity — the water vapour in the air — is measured by a hygrometer as relative humidity from dry (20–40%) to humid (60–80%), and these readings, alongside traditional signs such as ants moving eggs or frogs croaking, feed forecasts of rain, of the monsoon's arrival and IMD colour-coded heavy-rain warnings. |
| 7 | [[social_sciences-vii-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Climates of India]] | ◐ extends | The monsoon is a seasonal wind reversal — in summer the heated Asian landmass forms a low that draws moisture-laden ocean winds inland to drop heavy rain (the south-west monsoon advancing from early June to cover India by mid-July, the Western Ghats leaving the Deccan in a rain shadow, Mawsynram receiving about 11,000 mm a year), while in winter dry winds blow from land to sea except where the north-east monsoon gathers Bay of Bengal moisture for the east and south — and a failed monsoon means scarcity, higher food prices and migration. |
| 7 | [[social_sciences-vii-ch01|Social Sciences · Ch 1 Geographical Diversity of India]] | ○ uses | Rainfall varies from extremely scarce in the cold desert of Ladakh to among the highest in the world in the Garo, Khasi and Jaintia Hills of Meghalaya. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch06|Science · Ch 6 Pressure, Winds, Storms, and Cyclones]] | ◐ extends | Storms form when warm moist air rises, expands and cools so its water vapour condenses into clouds and the droplets merge to fall as rain, hail or snow; ice particles and droplets rubbing inside the cloud build the charges that discharge as lightning in a thunderstorm (Kalboishakhi, Bordoisila and mango showers are pre-monsoon examples), and cyclones bring intense winds and heavy rainfall around a calm eye. |
| 9 | [[science-ix-ch13|Science · Ch 13 Earth as a System: Energy, Matter, and Life]] | ◐ extends | Nearly all weather happens in the troposphere, and the monsoon is now treated as climate-sensitive: warmer Arabian Sea water intensifies the southwest monsoon, deforestation lowers local rainfall, and IITM Pune's coupled atmosphere–ocean–land–ice models forecast how warming may alter monsoon rainfall. |
| 9 | [[social_sciences-ix-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Atmosphere and Climate]] | ◐ extends | Precipitation falls when saturated air condenses, shaped by prevailing winds, mountains and seasons; India's tropical monsoon climate has four IMD seasons, and the monsoon is a wind reversal — the south-west monsoon (June–September), drawn in by a low over the fast-heating landmass, brings most of India's rain, while the north-east monsoon (October–February) blows seaward and rains mainly on the south-east coast after crossing the Bay of Bengal — mapped by normal dates of advance and retreat, vital to farming, water supply and festivals, and studied by the National Monsoon Mission. |
| 9 | [[social_sciences-ix-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Shaping of the Earth's Surface]] | ○ uses | Heavy continuous rainfall adds weight and reduces friction on slopes to trigger landslides and fills glacial lakes before outbursts, while prolonged drought and low rainfall leave loose dry soil for dust storms. |
