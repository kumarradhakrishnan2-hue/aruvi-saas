# The water cycle

*Evaporation → clouds → precipitation → collection; the Sun as the driver; the cycle as a system.*

Part of the [[Water]] thread.

**First taught:** Class 3, [[the_world_around_us-iii-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Water–A Precious Gift]].

## The idea, class by class

| Class | Subject · chapter | Role | What this chapter says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | [[the_world_around_us-iii-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Water–A Precious Gift]] | ● introduces | After rain falls it soaks into the soil, runs into streams and rivers, collects in puddles, ponds and lakes, or is stored underground as groundwater that feeds wells — the cycle grounded at a concrete, observable level without the formal 'water cycle' term. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch01|The World Around Us · Ch 1 Water — The Essence of Life]] | ● introduces | The Sun's heat evaporates water from oceans and rivers, the vapour cools into clouds, and rain, snow and hail fall back into rivers, lakes and oceans in a continuous cycle, modelled with coloured water sealed in a transparent bag left in sunlight. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch08|Science · Ch 8 A Journey through States of Water]] | ● introduces | Water vapour rising from oceans, lakes and rivers cools and condenses around dust particles to form clouds, the droplets merge into drops heavy enough to fall as rain (or hail or snow), and the water collects again in oceans, lakes, rivers and groundwater — the water cycle. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Oceans and Continents]] | ○ uses | Oceans drive the water cycle by generating the rainfall that keeps continents from being deserts, which is why clouds forming over the ocean are the source of India's summer monsoon. |
| 7 | [[science-vii-ch07|Science · Ch 7 Heat Transfer in Nature]] | ◐ extends | Solar radiation drives evaporation from oceans, rivers and lakes and transpiration from plants, the rising vapour cools and condenses into clouds, and precipitation as rain, snow or hail returns water through soil, rocks and plants to the water bodies — with water present in nature as liquid, as snow, ice sheets and glaciers, and as vapour. |
| 7 | [[social_sciences-vii-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Understanding the Weather]] | ○ uses | The IMD's motto 'From the sun arises rain', taken from the Manusmṛiti's 'from the sun arises rain, from rain comes food, and from food, living beings originate', is presented as the ancient understanding of the sun's role in the water cycle. |
| 7 | [[social_sciences-vii-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Climates of India]] | ○ uses | Cooler, moisture-laden winds drawn in from the ocean condense their moisture over the heated land and drop it as heavy monsoon rain, and winds crossing the Bay of Bengal collect moisture again for east and south India — the ocean-to-rain leg of the water cycle. |
| 8 | [[science-viii-ch13|Science · Ch 13 Our Home: Earth, a Unique Life Sustaining Planet]] | ○ uses | Water vapour forms clouds that bring rain and snow, refilling rivers and lakes and deciding which plants and animals can live in a region — and cutting down a forest can change the rainfall. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch08|Social Sciences · Ch 8 World Geography: Some Glimpses]] | ○ uses | Oceans add moisture to the air that forms clouds and rainfall, which is how the distant Southern Indian Ocean influences the monsoon in the Gangetic Plains. |
| 9 | [[science-ix-ch13|Science · Ch 13 Earth as a System: Energy, Matter, and Life]] | ◐ extends | The water cycle now includes surface runoff back to the ocean and infiltration into soil and rock, where water dissolves and carries minerals, and it links all five spheres — so a warmer atmosphere holding more moisture brings heavier rains in some places and droughts in others, while intense rainfall erodes soil and cuts groundwater recharge. |
| 9 | [[social_sciences-ix-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Atmosphere and Climate]] | ○ uses | Most water vapour and clouds sit in the troposphere, and the monsoon winds carry moisture gathered over the Indian Ocean, Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal onto the land as rain. |
