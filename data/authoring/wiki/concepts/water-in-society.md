# Water in society

*Civilisations by rivers, water in architecture, urban supply, community disputes, water in stories.*

Part of the [[Water]] thread.

**First taught:** Class 3, [[the_world_around_us-iii-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Water–A Precious Gift]].

## The idea, class by class

| Class | Subject · chapter | Role | What this chapter says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | [[the_world_around_us-iii-ch07|The World Around Us · Ch 7 Water–A Precious Gift]] | ● introduces | People have long stored water in clay, copper, brass and other traditional vessels, kept pots of water outside their homes for passers-by in the hot months, and celebrate rain and rivers in songs and poems. |
| 3 | [[english-iii-ch13|English · Ch 13 Madhu's Wish]] | ○ uses | In Sudha Murty's story a magical tree grants Madhu every kind of food but no water, and the tale's whole moral is that water is worth more than any feast. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch02|The World Around Us · Ch 2 Journey of a River]] | ◐ extends | Since ancient times settlements have grown beside rivers for reliable water; the Godavari is sacred (Dakshina Ganga, the Pushkaram festival at Nashik), families carry river stories and festivals, and students role-play as Sarpanch or plan a riverside city that stays clean, flood-safe and supplied through summer. |
| 5 | [[english-v-ch06|English · Ch 6 What a Tank!]] | ○ uses | Rural Punjab's rooftop water tanks are sculpted from cement and steel into flowers, footballs, roosters, tractors and aeroplanes that announce the owner's occupation or passion, and children design a community poster to preserve and clean old water tanks. |
| 5 | [[english-v-ch08|English · Ch 8 The Decision of the Panchayat]] | ○ uses | A village Panchayat under the banyan tree settles a dispute in which a neighbour who sold a well claims he did not sell the water in it, ruling that water kept in another's well belongs to the well's owner, and children judge whether the decision was fair. |
| 5 | [[the_world_around_us-v-ch01|The World Around Us · Ch 1 Water — The Essence of Life]] | ○ uses | Communities have built traditional rainwater-harvesting structures such as the johads of Rajasthan (small dams that collect rain and recharge groundwater), and Hiware Bazar in Maharashtra became water-positive through community-led watershed management. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch06|Social Sciences · Ch 6 The Beginnings of Indian Civilisation]] | ● introduces | India's first civilisation grew on the plains watered by the Indus and the Sarasvatī, and its planned cities counted water and drainage management as a mark of urbanism — the waterproofed Great Bath of Mohenjo-daro, hundreds of brick-lined wells, and Dholavira's six stone or rock-cut reservoirs (the largest 73 m long) linked by underground drains. |
| 6 | [[science-vi-ch11|Science · Ch 11 Nature's Treasures]] | ○ uses | Rainwater harvesting is an age-old Indian practice, seen in traditional stepwells — the Bawadi of Rajasthan and the Vav of Gujarat — that store rainwater and water seeping from nearby ponds and lakes. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Oceans and Continents]] | ○ uses | From earliest times people have used seas and oceans to migrate, trade, wage military campaigns and fish, coastal cultures built tales and legends around the sea, and ocean-born tsunamis such as the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami are now met with early warning systems and the National Disaster Management Authority. |
| 6 | [[social_sciences-vi-ch03|Social Sciences · Ch 3 Landforms and Life]] | ○ uses | The first civilisations grew up around rivers in fertile plains, river sources and confluences are held sacred with festivals and rituals in India, rivers carry navigation and trade, and the Sangam tiṇai Neydal ties coastal life to fishing and seafaring. |
| 7 | [[english-vii-ch01|English · Ch 1 The Day the River Spoke (Learning Together)]] | ○ uses | The River is a living character who listens to a village girl and counsels her to go to school, and children go on to discuss why India's rivers are held sacred and mostly addressed as female (the Brahmaputra and Sone being male) and to name the country's boats — canoe, catamaran, coracle, shikara — and other water-transport words (boats placed here as the nearest fit in the vocabulary). |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch10|Social Sciences · Ch 10 A Journey Through Indian Architecture]] | ◐ extends | Water structures are a distinct strand of Indian architecture: from Harappan drainage, Dholavira's reservoirs and interlocking trapezoid-brick wells, through Shringaverpur's Ganga-fed chain of reservoirs with silt-settling tanks, to the stepwells (vāvs, bāorīs such as Rani ki Vav) of Gujarat and Rajasthan that stored rainwater and gave a cool social space, temple tanks (puṣhkariṇīs) for harvesting and ritual, the Grand Anicut, and Agra Fort's Persian wheels lifting Yamuna water to fountains that cooled the palace. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch14|Social Sciences · Ch 14 India's Urban Landscape]] | ◐ extends | Cities have always depended on water — places near rivers grew into trade centres, Harappan Dholavira had complex water structures, and Jaipur was laid out in 1727 with reservoirs and canals for a steady supply in a dry climate — and today water supply and sewerage are core problems of urban planning, eased in Delhi by spreading growth across the National Capital Region and built into Singapore's master plans and Ahmedabad's Sabarmati Riverfront. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch01|Social Sciences · Ch 1 Natural Resources and Their Use]] | ○ uses | Water resources that cross political boundaries generate inter-state and international tensions, as with the Kaveri River shared among Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Puducherry. |
| 8 | [[social_sciences-viii-ch08|Social Sciences · Ch 8 World Geography: Some Glimpses]] | ○ uses | River valleys nurtured some of the world's earliest civilisations — the Nile was the cradle of ancient Egypt — and waterways carry trade: the Persian Gulf linked India and Europe for millennia, bays shelter ports and dockyards, and the Suez Canal (1869) made the shortest sea route from India to Europe. |
| 9 | [[social_sciences-ix-ch02|Social Sciences · Ch 2 Shaping of the Earth's Surface]] | ○ uses | The river plains of the Ganga, Nile, Brahmaputra and Indus gave rise to agricultural societies and early cities, coasts and harbours supported trade and cultural contact, ancient water management is documented in the Vedas, Kṛiṣhiparāśhara and Kauṭilya's Arthaśhāstra, and waterfalls and karst caves carry cultural or religious significance. |
