# The Invisible Living World: Beyond Our Naked Eye

**Science · Class 8 · Chapter 2**

*Cells, levels of organisation and microorganisms are introduced through the microscope, with microbes' roles in decomposition, food and water.*

> The chapter opens by explaining that the human eye cannot see objects below a certain size, and that the invention of the lens — shaped like a lentil seed — and subsequently the microscope, revealed a hidden world of tiny living creatures. Robert Hooke (1665) used a microscope to observe cork slices, naming the compartments he saw "cells." Around the same time Antonie van Leeuwenhoek built improved microscopes and became the first to clearly see and describe bacteria and blood cells, earning him the title Father …

## Water ideas in this chapter

*None recorded.* Water appears only as a sampling medium (pond or stagnant water observed under the microscope, a water-filled flask used as a lens) and as the habitat of microalgae; the chapter teaches cells and microorganisms, not any water concept.
