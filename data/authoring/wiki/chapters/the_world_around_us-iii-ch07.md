# Water–A Precious Gift

**The World Around Us · Class 3 · Chapter 7**

*Children observe rain, follow where rainwater goes, trace where their household water comes from and how it is stored, and learn that every drop must be saved.*

## Water ideas in this chapter

| Idea | Role | What this chapter says | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[rain-and-monsoon|Rain and the monsoon]] | ● introduces | Rain is met by direct observation: children read sky and nature clues to predict whether it will rain, then watch a shower and describe its heaviness, drop size, speed and slant, and collect rainwater to see whether it looks clean or dirty. | Section: Here Comes the Rain! / T-1, T-2, T-3 |
| [[water-cycle|The water cycle]] | ● introduces | After rain falls it soaks into the soil, runs into streams and rivers, collects in puddles, ponds and lakes, or is stored underground as groundwater that feeds wells — the cycle grounded at a concrete, observable level without the formal 'water cycle' term. | Section: What happened to the rainwater? / T-4, T-5, T-19 |
| [[oceans-and-water-bodies|Oceans and water bodies]] | ● introduces | Streams, rivers, ponds and lakes are the local water bodies where rainwater collects, and students find one near their village or city, visit and draw it, and record whether its water is (or was) used for drinking. | Section: What happened to the rainwater? / T-8; Let us reflect / T-20 |
| [[water-as-resource|Water as a resource]] | ● introduces | Tap water comes from rooftop tanks, pipes, wells, borewells or tankers; many people must carry water from a distant well, river or lake; households store water against irregular supply; and water is precious, so it must not be wasted or polluted. | Sections: Water is a Precious Gift! / Water in Our Daily Lives / Every Drop Counts (T-10 to T-15, T-22) |
| [[water-in-society|Water in society]] | ● introduces | People have long stored water in clay, copper, brass and other traditional vessels, kept pots of water outside their homes for passers-by in the hot months, and celebrate rain and rivers in songs and poems. | Sections: Water is a Precious Gift! / Water in Our Daily Lives / Every Drop Counts (T-6, T-9, T-14, T-17) |
| [[water-and-life|Water and life]] | ○ uses | Water is essential for the survival of people, birds and animals, so students build and maintain a birdbath, note which birds and insects come to drink, and ask whether the water near them is fit for drinking. | Section: Every Drop Counts / T-18, T-21; Let us reflect / T-20 |
| [[measuring-water|Measuring water]] | ○ uses | Students estimate how much water everyday tasks need in informal units (mugs of water to brush teeth, water used for a shower) to build a sense of quantity — placed under the nearest id, since no standard units such as litres are used. | Section: Water in Our Daily Lives / T-16 |
