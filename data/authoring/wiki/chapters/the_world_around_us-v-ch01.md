# Water — The Essence of Life

**The World Around Us · Class 5 · Chapter 1**

*Freshwater is a tiny share of Earth's water; the chapter builds the three forms of water, the water cycle, groundwater, river flow and freshwater habitats through models and a field visit.*

## Water ideas in this chapter

| Idea | Role | What this chapter says | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[oceans-and-water-bodies|Oceans and water bodies]] | ● introduces | Most of Earth's surface is covered by water but nearly all of it is salty ocean water unfit for drinking or farming; freshwater is only a tiny fraction (a teaspoon in a glass) held in rivers, ponds, lakes and underground, and India's rivers drain either to the Bay of Bengal or the Arabian Sea. | Section: Water on Earth / T-1, T-2, T-3; Surface Water / T-10; Let us reflect / T-16 |
| [[water-cycle|The water cycle]] | ● introduces | The Sun's heat evaporates water from oceans and rivers, the vapour cools into clouds, and rain, snow and hail fall back into rivers, lakes and oceans in a continuous cycle, modelled with coloured water sealed in a transparent bag left in sunlight. | Section: Water Cycle / T-5 |
| [[water-as-resource|Water as a resource]] | ● introduces | Rainwater percolates through soil and rock to become groundwater drawn up by wells, borewells, tube wells and handpumps; paved city surfaces block recharge while open ground, soak pits, ponds and trees restore it, and students design a house or school that conserves water. | Section: Groundwater / T-6, T-7; Let us reflect / T-18, T-19, T-20 |
| [[rivers-and-landforms|Rivers and landforms]] | ● introduces | Rivers begin in mountains and flow downhill with their path and speed shaped by the land they cross (and flowing water reshapes land over time), modelled with mustard seeds on crumpled paper and traced on a map of India's rivers, including the Luni that ends in the Rann of Kutch without reaching a sea. | Section: Surface Water / T-8, T-9, T-10 |
| [[water-and-life|Water and life]] | ● introduces | All living beings depend on freshwater, and freshwater habitats hold specially adapted plants and animals (fins and gills, waxy leaves; dragonfly, pond heron, freshwater turtle, lotus, water hyacinth) linked in a food chain, which students observe on a visit to a pond, lake or aquarium. | Section: Life in Water / T-11, T-12, T-13, T-14; Let us reflect / T-17 |
| [[states-of-water|States of water]] | ◐ extends | Water exists as liquid (rain), solid (ice) and vapour (steam); students watch droplets condense on a cold steel glass of ice, ice melt, water boil and water left in sunlight for three days disappear, recording that water changes form and can return to liquid. | Section: Water has Different Forms / T-4; Let us reflect / T-15 |
| [[water-in-society|Water in society]] | ○ uses | Communities have built traditional rainwater-harvesting structures such as the johads of Rajasthan (small dams that collect rain and recharge groundwater), and Hiware Bazar in Maharashtra became water-positive through community-led watershed management. | Sections: Water on Earth; Surface Water |
