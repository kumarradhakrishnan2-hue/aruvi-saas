Lifting the cards off the paper

Not a problem of telling classes apart — six cards fit on one phone screen and the section letter already carries the identity. The problem is that the card and the page are nearly the same plane.

Measured, and worse than it looks — the green card is the faintest thing on the screen. Contrast against the page (#faf7f1): ongoing green 1.04 : 1 · unstarted grey 1.10 : 1 · complete terracotta 1.17 : 1. Anything under about 1.2 does not read as a separate surface at all, so today the card is held up almost entirely by its 1px border. And the ranking is upside down: the card she is actually teaching from separates least, the one she has finished with separates most.

Two dials, and the cheap one is the border. Darkening the fill trades against the muted text on it ("Pick a chapter to begin"), which is at 4.61 : 1 today and has almost no room left. Firming the 1px edge costs nothing at all — today it is #ece7dc, which sits at 1.05 : 1 against its own fill and is very nearly invisible. Each level below moves both, and nudges the muted ink just enough to hold 4.5 : 1.

What I would build: level 2, and the edge change at any level. The fills are the visible half, but the border is where the separation is actually being lost — a 1.05 : 1 edge is not an edge. Giving each state an edge in its own hue is what makes the card read as a plane rather than as a slightly different patch of paper.

Scope, and one thing to check. These would be new surface tokens on the card (--card-new / --card-going / --card-done plus their edges), NOT changes to --paper-sunk and --tint-pine — those two are used in 37 and 35 other places and would drag half the app with them. My Lessons deliberately shares this status palette, so it moves too, which is right. The dark theme needs its own three values measured the same way against #161d19; a light-mode lift does not transfer.
