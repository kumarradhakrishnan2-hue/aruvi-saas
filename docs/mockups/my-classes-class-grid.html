Telling classes apart without spending the colour

Today the section card has three colours and they all mean one thing: grey = nothing attached, green = teaching it now, terracotta = finished. That is the status channel, and it works because it is the only thing colour says.

Does it make sense? Yes — and for a sharper reason than "too many colours confuse". Colour on this screen is already spoken for. If a fourth, fifth and sixth colour arrive to mean "this is 6B", then a green card no longer reliably means "teaching it now" — it might mean "Class 7". The two meanings would corrupt each other, and status is the one thing she must read without thinking.

But "grid" should mean POSITION, not PATTERN. A hatch or dot texture per class (option C below) is the literal reading, and it costs her a key to memorise — after four patterns they blur worse than colours did. Grouping the list puts each class in its own place, which needs no legend at all, scales to any number of classes, and survives dark mode and colour-blindness untouched.

What I would build. B, and nothing else — the marginal rail is the same device the lesson view already uses for period 01 and question Q1, so it arrives as something she has met rather than as a new code. It also removes a repetition: the class number is said once for the group instead of on every card, which is what buys back the 56px the margin costs.

One thing to decide before it is built: what the groups are ordered by, and whether an ongoing class should float to the top. Right now the list is in profile order. Grouping makes that order visible — and therefore worth choosing on purpose.
