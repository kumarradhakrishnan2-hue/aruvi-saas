  • Student evidence worksheet, one per student, printed landscape (see visual aid: 'Student evidence worksheet — tide pool census')

  • Blackboard or whiteboard for the whole-class census tally

Hermit crabA small creature occupies the hollow of a smooth spiral shell, and when touched the opening is pulled firmly inward and sealed. The shell itself is dull and shows no change during the observation period.
Sea anemoneA soft, column-shaped form is fixed to the surface of a rock, and its upper end carries a ring of slender, waving tentacles that close around any small particle drifting into contact. The body does not shift position on the rock during the observation period.
SeaweedA rubbery, branching mass of greenish-brown fronds is anchored to a submerged stone by a disc at its base, and newer fronds near the tips are noticeably paler and smaller than older ones further down. The fronds sway continuously as water moves around them.
BarnacleA pale, cone-shaped casing is cemented firmly to a stone, and at intervals a feathery set of limbs fans out through a gap at the top and sweeps back inside. Between these sweeps the casing shows no movement at all.
Dry coral skeletonA brittle, white, branching structure rests loosely on the pool floor, chalky to the touch and lighter than it looks. No part of it changes position or appearance over the full observation period.
Small fishA streamlined creature moves rapidly through the water, changes direction sharply when a hand is placed near the pool, and can be seen regularly opening and closing its mouth near the surface. Its scales catch the light as it turns.
PebbleA smooth, grey stone rests on the pool floor and remains completely still throughout the observation period. Its surface is cool and evenly coloured, with no visible openings, textures of growth, or change of any kind.
